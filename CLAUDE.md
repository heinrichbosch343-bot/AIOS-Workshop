## AIOS Mission

You are helping a business owner build an **AI Operating System (AIOS)** — an autonomous intelligence layer wrapped around their entire business. Everything in this workspace serves that goal.

### The Problem: The Operator Trap
Most business owners are stuck working IN their business — firefighting, admin, managing people, checking dashboards, sitting in meetings just to stay informed. 80% of bandwidth goes to "must-dos." Nothing left for growth, strategy, or the life they actually wanted. The old model says hire more people, buy more tools, work more hours. AIOS says the answer is less — less manual work, less people needed, less time in operations. More bandwidth for the work that matters.

### The Solution: Five Layers
The AIOS gives it back — one layer at a time:
1. **Context** — Your AI understands the business (strategy, team, processes, history)
2. **Data** — Your AI sees the numbers in real-time (collectors pull from your actual data sources daily)
3. **Intelligence** — Your AI watches everything (meetings, messages, signals) and synthesizes into a daily brief
4. **Automate** — Audit every task, score each one, automate them away one by one. Each task automated = bandwidth recovered.
5. **Build** — Freed bandwidth applied to growth, new initiatives, or life. Work ON the business, not IN it.

### Five Principles
1. **Just Ask** — If you can describe it in plain English, Claude can build it. Don't self-censor. Ask for the impossible.
2. **Talk, Don't Type** — Voice-first. Hold FN, speak for 60 seconds, let Claude format it. 3x faster than typing.
3. **Layers, Not Leaps** — One layer at a time. Each independently valuable. Through gradual exposure, you become technical without even trying.
4. **Build for Scale & Security** — Human-in-the-loop by default. Your data stays local. Plan before you build.
5. **Borrow Before You Build** — 80% modules, 20% custom. Check the library before building from scratch.

### Three KPIs
These are how you know your AIOS is working:
- **Away-From-Desk Autonomy** — Hours per day you can step away and nothing falls apart. Target: business runs while you sleep.
- **Task Automation %** — Percentage of recurring tasks automated. Use the Task Audit (`context/task-audit.md`) as your scoreboard.
- **Revenue Per Employee** — Total revenue ÷ team members. Not bigger companies — leaner, faster, more profitable ones.

### How You Should Help
- Be patient. Assume the user is non-technical unless told otherwise.
- **Every chat reply is short and simple, like a teacher explaining it, whatever the topic (technical, business, sales, anything).** A few plain sentences, the one or two facts that actually matter, no jargon left unexplained. Heinrich said (20 Oct 2026) that a longer reply costs him four minutes and he skips 80% of it, so do the full work quietly and hand back only the short version. If he wants more, he'll ask. This does not apply to a deliverable he actually asked for (a plan, a PDF, a report, an artifact) — only to how Claude talks to him in chat.
- Celebrate wins — every module installed, every task automated is real progress toward freedom.
- When suggesting solutions, check existing modules and the community first (Borrow Before You Build).
- Keep the three KPIs in mind — every automation should move at least one KPI.
- Never dump error logs or technical jargon. Find the problem, explain it simply, fix it.

## Commands

### /install [module-path]

**Purpose:** Install an AIOS module into this workspace.

Point it at a module folder in `module-installs/` and Claude walks you through the guided setup. Each module adds a new capability to your AIOS.

Example: `/install module-installs/context-os`

### /prime

**Purpose:** Initialize a new session with full context awareness.

Run this at the start of every session. Claude will:

1. Read CLAUDE.md and context files
2. Summarize understanding of the user, workspace, and goals
3. Confirm readiness to assist

### /create-plan [request]

**Purpose:** Create a detailed implementation plan before making changes.

Use when adding new functionality, commands, scripts, or making structural changes. Produces a thorough plan document in `plans/` that captures context, rationale, and step-by-step tasks.

Example: `/create-plan add a competitor analysis command`

### /implement [plan-path]

**Purpose:** Execute a plan created by /create-plan.

Reads the plan, executes each step in order, validates the work, and updates the plan status.

Example: `/implement plans/2026-01-28-competitor-analysis-command.md`

### /share [system or feature]

**Purpose:** Package a system or feature from your workspace for sharing.

Deep-dives the code first to fully understand it, then produces a self-contained, beginner-friendly package with a Claude-guided installer (INSTALL.md + README.md + scripts). The recipient gives the folder to Claude Code and says "read INSTALL.md and set this up" — Claude walks them through everything step by step. Runs a 6-stage interactive flow: Research → Scope → Frame → Write → Validate → Deliver. Outputs to `shares/`.

Example: `/share the daily brief system`

### /process

**Purpose:** Process the GTD inbox to zero using the decision tree.

Routes each captured item to projects, next-actions, waiting-for, someday-maybe, or trash. Refreshes the dashboard when done.

### /review

**Purpose:** Run a guided GTD weekly review (30-60 minutes).

Walks through GET CLEAR → GET CURRENT → GET CREATIVE → REBUILD. Processes inbox, reviews all project and action lists, scans areas and someday-maybe, updates the dashboard. Run weekly (Fridays recommended).

### /brainstorm [topic]

**Purpose:** Workspace scanner and opportunity finder.

Scans your tasks, processes, and current setup to find manual work that could be automated. Ranks opportunities by impact and feasibility, deep-dives the top picks, and points you to `/explore` or `/implement` for the next step. Run without arguments to scan everything, or with a topic to focus on a specific area.

**Rebuilt on 2026-08-28 around `rfp-system-ad`, which is now the scaffold source** (`stanford-canaries-ad` stays the ENGINE source of truth that `motion_sync.py` compares against, and nothing else scaffolds from it). Five things changed and all five are in `.claude/commands/motion.md`:

- **Boschly violet, not cyan.** Ground `#0A0A0C`, accent `#B14AE8`, lit `#E0A8FF`, straight off the site's own tokens. The palette keys in `theme.ts` still spell `cyan`, deliberately, because `src/primitives/` is byte-synced to canaries and renaming the accent slot would desync every project permanently. Electric cyan `#33E1FF` is retired.

**The brand layer (added 2026-08-28) is the second half of that job**, and it is what makes a video look like the website rather than merely share its accent. `outputs/content/rfp-system-ad/src/theme.ts` now carries the site's real tokens from `alacrity-site/app/globals.css` + `lib/tokens.ts`, and scenes import from it rather than hardcoding: **General Sans** (the site's own face, a local woff2 in `public/fonts/`, loaded with `FontFace` + `delayRender` since it is not a Google font — Inter is only the fallback now) and **JetBrains Mono** for eyebrows; **ember gold `#C89A3C` / `#F5A83A` as the second accent**, which the site uses 21 times and every build so far used zero (`.eyebrow` on the site is always gold, so the `kicker` export is now mono + gold and its colour must not be overridden); a **lifted ground** (`GROUND`, the site's `radial-gradient(58% 54% at 50% 44%, #101014, #0A0A0C)` — flat black is a rectangle, the lift is a lit stage); **hairlines as light at low alpha** (`C.line` / `C.lineFaint`) rather than the solid `#2E2636`, taking the site's *strong* 0.24 value because a 10% line holds on a desktop display and is eaten by x264 on a phone; **panels as a wash** (`PANEL`) over the void with a hairline, never a filled lighter box; and **a shape vocabulary of exactly three** (`R.card` 5px, `R.dot` a circle, `R.pill`) replacing the twelve ad-hoc radii that were a large part of why the builds still read as generic. Weights run one step under the old Inter values (500 headline / 400 sub / 600 numbers) because General Sans sits heavier at the same number; **the site's own 300 body weight is deliberately not copied**, since it holds on a page someone sits with and vanishes on a muted phone video. `FONT` and `C` are single exports read by the locked primitives too, so the face and palette change from one file and `motion_sync.py` stays clean. **Scaffolding a new build must copy `public/fonts/` as well as `src/`** — without it the render silently falls back to Segoe UI.
- **Pace, and it was measured.** Voice speed **1.20** (ElevenLabs' hard ceiling; 1.22 is a 400) with PAD_SECONDS **0.10**, landing 222 to 240 wpm. Every build before this one came in under 208 wpm, which is below the floor of all seven videos in the swipe corpus. Heinrich's note on the slow cut was simply "it's very slow".
- **Two timelines.** `SCENES` carries audio and captions, `SHOTS` carries the picture and cuts wherever it likes off `cueFrame()`, roughly 2.4 seconds each. Splitting the narration itself per shot makes the model read every fragment with a falling full-stop intonation.
- **The whole layout is verified free.** A `silent` prop plus a `ShotsOnly` composition renders all 27 shots against estimated timings before a single TTS credit is spent.

**And the stills are now measured, not only looked at (added 2026-09-08): `scripts/frame_lab.py`.** It is to the picture what `hook_lab.py` is to the script — deterministic, no model call, no key, no network, no cost — so it runs on every still in the ShotsOnly pass without anyone thinking about the bill. It exits non-zero on five defects: **DEAD** (a shot that renders nothing, which is what a failed `cueFrame()` match looks like), **SPLIT** (two subjects far apart, and at 2.4 seconds a shot the eye does not choose), **DIFFUSE** (attention scattered over three or more places), **MARGIN** (real content inside the 90px safe margin) and **CAPTION** (lit content where the caption band will land — the ShotsOnly pass is the only place that collision is visible before the read is paid for, because the band is empty there and full later). `--overlay` writes the frame with its attention map in gold, the safe margin drawn and the caption band shaded, which is the output actually worth reading.

```bash
uv run --no-project --system-certs --with numpy --with pillow \
  python scripts/frame_lab.py --project outputs/content/{topic}-ad --overlay
uv run ... python scripts/frame_lab.py --project <p> --with-captions --overlay   # Step 8, full stills
```

**It is bottom-up saliency, not a brain model and not a performance predictor.** The attention map is spectral residual (Hou & Zhang, CVPR 2007 — log-amplitude of the FFT minus its own smoothed copy, which suppresses whatever repeats and leaves whatever does not, so a panel of twelve identical document lines scores low and the one violet pill inside it scores high) mixed 45/55 with lit mass, because on a near-black ground an area with no light cannot pull the eye however odd its spectrum. It answers a layout question that has a right answer; it says nothing about whether the video travels, and a clean run means nothing obvious is broken rather than that the shot is good.

**Every threshold was measured off the 20 shipped stills of `rfp-system-ad`, not guessed**, and the measurement sits next to the constant: ground plus grain is p50 0.0044 in linear relative luminance, so the lit line is 0.020 and the focal line 0.050. Three calibration facts that each cost a pass to find — regions are cut by **percentile, not by a share of the map's maximum** (the map has a floor, so dividing by a total that includes it made the wide `YearTrack` block read as 5% attention when it is plainly one object); the map is **blurred again before regions are cut**, so thirteen bars 50px apart group into one object the way the eye groups them; and **`--with-captions` must remove the band from the attention map as well as the margin test**, because burnt-in caption type is always a second subject and sits at `left: 64`, inside the 90px margin by design, so without it every full still reports both a split and a breach.

**Pointed at the scaffold build it found two real defects, both still open:** `YearTrack` and the closing question headline breach the 90px margin, and **gold is 0.00% on all 20 stills** — a rule motion.md states ("A build with no gold in it is off-brand") that nothing enforced until now.
- **The comment pill is un-retired**, on the condition that it offers a demonstration and lands after the question. `context/linkedin/offers-and-funnels.md` is updated to match.

Delivery is **crf 27**, not 24: grain is per-frame noise x264 cannot predict, and the same master is 97.5 MB at 24 against 35 MB at 27 with no visible loss.

**The look layer** (added 2026-07-28, engine source `outputs/content/stanford-canaries-ad/src/`) is what stops these reading as stock AI explainers, and is mandatory on every new build:

- `easings.ts` — a motion curve per ROLE (`E.snap` type, `E.draw` data, `E.weight` objects, `E.camera` frame, `E.exit` everything leaving) plus `stagger()` and `useBreathe()`. One curve for a whole video is the loudest template tell.
- `camera.tsx` — `Camera` (push / pull / drift / rise, centred on 1.0 so nothing gets cropped), `Parallax` depth planes, `ScaleThrough` and `MaskWipe` and `Whip` in place of cross-fades. The frame is never locked off.
- `optical.tsx` — `Optics` overlay (film grain in shadow mode, halation, breathing vignette) plus per-element `Bloom` and `Fringe`. Drop `<Optics accent={...} />` as the last child of the master.
- `LookTest.tsx` — `LookBefore` / `LookAfter` compositions for A/B stills when tuning.


**The evidence is sorted by tier in `hook-doctrine.md`, and Tier C matters as much as Tier A.** Three papers hold up: Loewenstein 1994 (curiosity is a deprivation response to a gap made *salient*, so the gap must be in something the viewer has a stake in), Berger & Milkman 2012 (high-arousal emotion travels, sadness suppresses), Green & Brock 2000 (story absorption suppresses counter-arguing, which is why the zero-"you" anecdote beats a claim at a sceptical buyer). ## Meeting recorder (local, added 2026-09-16)

**The Meeting Recorder icon on the desktop → http://localhost:8515.** `scripts/meeting_app.py` (Streamlit), launched by `meeting.vbs` → `meeting.ps1`, which starts it only if it isn't already up (same pattern as the CRM and RFP icons; the icon image is `scripts/assets/meeting-recorder.ico`). One page: who it's with, language (**Detect it for me / English / Afrikaans**), an optional CRM contact picked by search, and a big **Record** button. While recording it shows a timer and live level bars for the mic and the PC audio, refreshed every second, so a muted mic shows up in the first second rather than after the call. **Stop and transcribe** hands off automatically. Below that is the recordings library (every session, its transcript, re-transcribe in another language, attach to a contact after the fact, open the folder). `scripts/start-meeting.bat` is the console fallback (`levels`, `devices`, `stop`, `status`).

**The page is only the controls; nothing depends on it staying open.** The recorder and the transcriber are detached processes (`ml.spawn_detached`, which breaks away from uv's job object), the recorder starts the transcriber itself when it stops, and all state lives in each session's `meta.json`. Closing the tab never loses a meeting. Output lands in `outputs/voice/meetings/<date>-<title>/` (`transcript.md`, `transcript.json`, `meta.json`, both WAVs), one line goes to `gtd/inbox.md`, and **if a contact was picked, a dated factual note ("Meeting recorded (32:10, Afrikaans). Transcript: …") is appended to them in `crm.csv` and mirrored to `activity-log.csv` as `action=note`, channel `meeting`.** That writer (`scripts/meeting_crm.py`) mirrors the dashboard's `stamp_note` exactly because importing `crm_dashboard.py` would launch Streamlit; it moves `last_touch` forward, never touches status, writes atomically, and refuses a ragged CSV rather than trimming it. The substance of the meeting stays `/meeting`'s job. Paths can be repointed with `AIOS_MEETINGS_DIR`, `AIOS_MEETINGS_INBOX`, `AIOS_CRM_CSV` and `AIOS_ACTIVITY_CSV`, which every child process inherits, and that is how the end-to-end test ran the real app, recorder and GPU without touching the real CRM.

**Nothing is uploaded, ever.** `large-v3-turbo` runs on the RTX 2060 SUPER at **14.6x to 23.2x realtime**, so an hour-long meeting takes a few minutes. Heinrich's instruction on 16 September was local only, always, which is the right call for a client talking candidly: no per-minute cost, no internet needed, and no POPIA question about who else holds the recording. The `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY` in `.env` is deliberately unused here. On the CPU the same model runs at **0.69x realtime**, so the GPU is not optional.

**Two tracks, not diarization, and that is the whole design.** Heinrich's voice arrives on the microphone; everyone else's comes out of the headphones and is captured separately through WASAPI loopback. So who said what is known by construction rather than guessed by a model, no pyannote and no gated download. Each track is also silent while the other person talks, and the silence filter skips silence, so both tracks of an hour cost about the same as one. The limit: anyone in the same room on the same mic is labelled as Heinrich, which is correct for a video call.

**The CUDA trap, which will come back if forgotten.** ctranslate2 fails with `Library cublas64_12.dll is not found`, and the obvious fix is a dead end: **`nvidia-cublas-cu12` and `nvidia-cudnn-cu12` are Linux-only wheels that install no DLLs at all on Windows.** The torch CUDA wheel does ship `cublas64_12.dll` and `cudnn64_9.dll` in `site-packages/torch/lib`, so **`torch` is a dependency purely to supply two DLLs**, registered with `os.add_dll_directory` *before* `faster_whisper` is imported. Resolve that path from `torch.__file__`, never from `sys.prefix`, which is wrong under uv. Also pin `--python 3.12`: the default here is 3.14.4 and the ML wheels don't support it. And `sounddevice` cannot do loopback at all (0.5.6 `WasapiSettings` has no such option), which is why this uses `soundcard`.

**The recorder refuses to start when the mic can't record, deliberately.** Finding out after a client call that your own voice was never recorded is the worst outcome available, because the meeting can't be repeated. The pre-flight (`ml.mic_problem`) tells three failures apart because they need different fixes: **not responding** (the device never returns a buffer; Windows had no working default input), **error**, and **muted** (a peak of exactly 0.0, which is what the Arctis returns when muted). **A quiet room passes**, because nobody is talking at the moment they click Record and any non-zero sample proves the mic is listening. The app then offers "Record PC audio only" (`--force`). The check waits 12 seconds, because an idle USB headset can take several to wake. On 16 September the mic went from not responding, to muted, to live (level 0.0085) as Heinrich worked through Sound settings, and it now records.

**A hung mic check appears to wedge the whole headset for about a minute.** Twice, a pre-flight that timed out was followed by a recording whose PC-audio track captured perfect silence while speech was demonstrably playing. The Arctis mic and headphones are one USB device. Launched from a clean state, the identical recorder and the identical launch flags captured both tracks every time (all four flag combinations were tested), so it isn't the launch. The app now warns when the PC-audio bar stays empty past 45 seconds and says to replug the headset. Treat it as the likely cause, observed twice, not proven.

**Three defects the first tests forced, all fixed and all worth knowing.** A recorder thread died silently at 17s of a 32s recording because the audio backend raised something `except Exception` doesn't catch, leaving the two tracks 15 seconds apart and every later turn merging into the wrong place, so the thread now catches `BaseException` and `pad_wav_to()` pads a short track to wall-clock length. Whisper returned segments running to 59 seconds on a 32-second file, so `clean_segments()` drops and clamps them and also filters the subtitle-style filler it invents from silence. And **whatever else is playing on the machine gets recorded** — one test transcript opened with a paragraph nobody said, which turned out to be real audio the loopback caught faithfully, confirmed by per-second levels.

**Call audio leaks into the headset mic, and Whisper turns the leak into words labelled Heinrich.** The first transcript that included the quiet mic track attributed "The proposal system is the part that heals us" to him, a garbled echo of the client's "the part that kills us". `ml.drop_bleed()` removes a mic segment only when **both** hold: the mic is near-silent over that window (RMS under 0.003) **and** the call track is at least 10x louder at that moment. Measured, not guessed: the leak sat at mic RMS 0.00022 to 0.00035 against call RMS 0.099 to 0.116, a ratio of about 0.002, and speech into a headset mic runs 0.01 and up. A soft word while the other side is quiet is kept, since nothing was playing to leak. The transcript header says how many fragments were removed. **Calibrated on playback with nobody speaking, so Heinrich's first real call is what confirms it keeps his own words.**

**Silence means a peak of exactly zero, not a low RMS.** The same run told Heinrich to check his mute button on a mic that was working (peak 0.0116, but almost no speech). `ml.track_is_silent()` now keys on the peak; a live-but-quiet mic gets "picked up almost no speech" with no mute advice and is still transcribed, which the silence filter makes cheap.

**`outputs/voice/vocabulary.txt` holds two things, and the prompt's style matters as much as its content.** Names become Whisper's initial prompt, **written as punctuated sentences**, because Whisper copies its prompt's style. Measured on one recording: no prompt gave full stops but "TenderPak"; the old bare comma list gave "TenderPack" but no full stops at all; sentences gave both. Lines with `=>` are **corrections** applied after transcription, for words the prompt can't fix (`Boschley | Bosch Lee => Boschly`, `BBBEE => B-BBEE`): whole words, case-insensitive, longest match first, and the replacement is literal. Add a line the first time a word comes back wrong.

Language is detected per track, one language per recording (Heinrich's decision: his thoughts come out in one language at a time), or chosen in the app. Detection is reliable: on his first real Afrikaans recording ("John test", 16 Sep) it read his voice as `af` at p=0.96 and an English video playing on the PC as `en` at p=1.00, and forcing `af` onto that English track did not garble it.

**Whisper writes Afrikaans in Dutch spelling, and only the correction list fixes it.** That first recording came back "bezig met een nieuwe project ... de plannen vir die toekomst". Three fixes were measured on it before building anything. An **Afrikaans-language prompt made zero difference** (word-for-word identical output), so the prompt stays English. **The full `large-v3` model was no better**: the same Dutch spellings, two new mistakes of its own ("bykie", "gehoorheid"), and 2.5x slower, so turbo stays. Only the **`af:` lines in `vocabulary.txt`** work (`af: bezig => besig`, `af: een nieuwe => 'n nuwe`, 27 of them). They apply **per sentence, not per track**: `ml.text_language()` counts Afrikaans against English function words, because a meeting set to Afrikaans can still carry English from the other side, and `af: project => projek` must never touch an English sentence. Only spellings that exist in Dutch and not in Afrikaans belong in that list. "het", "een" and "voor" are real Afrikaans words, so "het gaan" (should be "dit gaan") stays unfixed rather than breaking good sentences. **Two Afrikaans fine-tunes were then measured (16 Sep, second pass) on 92 seconds of Heinrich's real Afrikaans** (`john-test`, `meow`, `meeting`), every model run through `_transcribe_track` itself, mistakes counted against the best reading of what he said: **turbo with the word list 33, `deepdml/whisper-large-v3-turbo-af-mix-norm` 16, `andreoosthuizen/whisper-large-v3-afrikaans` 7.** The list can't keep up: the two new recordings still carried 29 mistakes after it ran, and some invert the meaning (turbo wrote "om nie seker te maak", to *not* make sure, for "om net seker te maak"). **deepdml is unsafe**: given the vocabulary prompt it dropped a whole sentence and turned "twaalf duisend vyfhonderd vier en dertig rand" into "$2,534,000". **andreoosthuizen won** (CC-BY-4.0, so the author gets credited; a LoRA on large-v3 trained on ~50 hours of spontaneous sermon speech, closer to a meeting than deepdml's read speech). It gets "dit gaan", "omtrent", "bietjie" and "eintlik" right with no list, and with a *punctuated Afrikaans* prompt carrying the names it wrote "dis Heinrich hierso" where every other setup wrote "designregister" or "aanregisseur". Its costs: **lowercase with no punctuation** (no prompt brings it back), "n" for "'n", no diaeresis, numbers spelled out; 12-19x realtime against turbo's 34-40x; it **detects Afrikaans as Yiddish** (yi p=0.52), so turbo must keep doing detection; and it lowercases English, so English tracks stay on turbo. Two traps: its top-level `adapter_model.safetensors` is a **40-byte empty file**, the real weights are in `last-checkpoint/` (step 4500), and a normal load silently tests stock large-v3; and both models loaded together take 6.9 of the 8 GB of VRAM. ## Proposal generator base install

Built 2026-09-11 for the AAA program post. The empty per-client copy of
`tenderpack-master`: same application, same interface, no data, no keys, no
marketing surface. Verified end to end: clean `npm install`, production build,
2,420 tests, type-check.

**It exists in two places and the payload is byte-identical.**
`shares/proposal-generator-base/` is the raw folder to hand someone directly;
`module-installs/proposal-generator-v1/AIOS Proposal Generator/` is the same app
wrapped as an AIOS module, installed with
`/install "module-installs/proposal-generator-v1/AIOS Proposal Generator"`. The
module's INSTALL.md is the beginner-facing one written to the house format (FOR
CLAUDE / OVERVIEW / SCOPING / PREREQUISITES / phases); the copy inside the app
folder is the terse version for somebody who already knows what Supabase is.
Change the app in one place and copy it to the other, or they drift.

**Three ordering traps the module INSTALL.md calls out**, because each one fails
quietly: the user must sign in once BEFORE any document is loaded (signup creates
the workspace the loaders write into), Voyage is not optional (without it
retrieval degrades to keyword matching and the generator writes something
plausible, which looks like success), and the demo stamp stays on until the
profile is real and approved.

**The line the neutralisation drew, and it is the reusable decision.** Generic
nouns became RFP and proposal; South African proper nouns kept their names,
because the SBD forms, B-BBEE and the eTenders radar are statutory things and
renaming one only makes it wrong. They fire on `TENDER_SA` detection and sit
idle on any other document. Code identifiers were never touched, only strings a
user reads, which is why the whole suite still passes.

**Two loaders are the populate step.** `scripts/load-extraction.mjs` takes facts
into `extracted_fields` (every one needs a verbatim `source_excerpt` or it is
dropped, identity numbers are refused outright, everything lands `pending` for a
human to approve). `scripts/load-content.mjs` is new and takes prose into
`content_library` (entries under 200 characters refused: a heading embeds to a
vector that sits near everything and matches everything). Then `npm run index`
calls Voyage. The split between them is the system's spine — a marketing
sentence with a field key on it gets approved by the same button as the B-BBEE
level and is pasted into a bid as a verified claim.

**`promptfoo` was in `dependencies` and is imported nowhere** — no config file, no
eval suite, no import in `src/` or `scripts/`. It is what drags in Playwright's
Chromium, `onnxruntime-node` and `better-sqlite3`, so dropping it took
`node_modules` from **2.1 GB to 987 MB** with 2,420 tests and a production build
still green. Removed in the base copies only; `tenderpack-master` still carries it.

**Windows path length kills the build and the error does not say so.** Turbopack
panics with `path length for file ... exceeds max length of filesystem` when the
app sits more than a few folders deep, because Windows caps a full path at 260
characters and the generated chunk names are long (`mermaid` produces the worst
of them). Keep the folder near the drive root. This is in the module INSTALL.md's
error handling, because a module people drop anywhere will hit it.

**Three defects in `tenderpack-master` surfaced on the way and are still there.**
`CLAUDE.md` carries a literal NUL byte, inside the paragraph explaining that a
NUL is invisible to every editor and review, so `grep` calls the file binary.
`package.json` lists neither `tsx` nor `dotenv` although `scripts/index-workspace.ts`
needs both, so a clean install breaks the indexer. And `supabase/STORAGE-SETUP.md`
instructs you to make the `proposal-library` bucket **public** with anon
insert/select/delete, while the app itself uses `createSignedUrl` — any install
that followed it published the client's certificates and past bids. All three are
fixed in the base copy only.

**2026-09-11, second pass: the gates came out.** Heinrich asked for no login, no
daily cap, no 60% lock and the statutory fields gone. Four things worth keeping
from doing it:

**Removing the login screen is not the same as removing the login.** Every table
is RLS-scoped to workspace membership through `auth.uid()`, so a signed-out
reader is shown nothing at all rather than everything. Deleting the sign-in page
alone produces an app that can never display a row however full the database is,
which reads as a broken install. `src/lib/supabase/server.ts` therefore returns a
**service-role client when `AUTH_ENABLED !== "true"`**, and that is the only
coherent way to run without a session. Set `AUTH_ENABLED=true` before any public
URL, which INSTALL.md now says twice.

**Signup was what created the workspace.** With nobody signing up, nothing did,
and every loader stopped with "No workspace exists yet". `src/lib/workspace/ensure-default.ts`
provisions one on the first dashboard visit. There is still a row in
`auth.users` because `workspaces.owner_id` is `NOT NULL REFERENCES auth.users(id)`
— a foreign key, not a login: the account is made through the admin API with a
password nobody is told.

**The cap that actually blocked work was the billing one, not the daily one.**
A single-tenant install has no Stripe row, so `canGenerateProposal` answered
"No active subscription" and refused every generation. All three limiters
(`rate-limit`, `usage-limit`, `billing/subscriptions`) now default to off;
`USAGE_LIMITS_ENABLED=1` puts them back. The **60% completeness gate was already
gone** before this pass, with a comment saying why: it measured the canonical
66-column profile this build leaves empty on purpose, read 5%, and blocked
generation on the most complete profile in the system.

**"Statutory fields" is what the dictation meant, not "stationary".** The fourth
tab on Company profile, 50 hand-typed columns feeding SBD 1 and SBD 4. Removing
it stranded the `profile_gap` attention item on the dashboard home, which pointed
at a form that no longer existed, so that went too. Taking the tab out left 11
dead symbols behind — the exact "existed in this file and were rendered nowhere"
trap the file had already been fixed for once — so the page was trimmed 877 to
561 lines and lints clean.

**Never build the share zip with PowerShell `Compress-Archive`.** It writes
Windows backslashes as path separators, which breaks the ZIP spec; `unzip` warns
and macOS can extract it as 533 files with backslashes in their names instead of
a folder tree. Build it with the `jszip` already in the app dependencies, writing
forward slashes. Four pre-existing `react-hooks/set-state-in-effect` lint errors
(`brand-zone`, `dashboard-shell`, `checklist`, `use-subscription`) are inherited
from `tenderpack-master` and are not from this work.

**Why Yahoo, measured rather than assumed.** From this machine with a browser-grade TLS client (`primp`): DuckDuckGo 403 / anomaly challenge, Google a JavaScript-only page, Brave 429, Startpage captcha, Mojeek 403, and **Bing served decoy results** (Swiss file-transfer sites for a LinkedIn query). **The `za.` subdomain is the country filter.** LinkedIn serves a South African member's public profile on za.linkedin.com, so `site:za.linkedin.com/in` returns South Africans only. Nobody has to guess a country from prose.

**Yahoo throttles a burst, and the throttle looks like success.** About twenty quick searches and it returns HTTP 200 with "We had temporary problems searching for web pages", no results, for a minute or two. `parse_serp()` treats anything that is neither results nor Yahoo's own "did not find results" wording as throttled, and **a throttled page is never cached** (caching it would record an outage as a genuinely empty search, forever). Searches are spaced 5-9 seconds; a throttle waits 1, 2, then 4 minutes as a fresh browser profile, then the run stops. Four things drive the shape and each is a real finding, not a preference. **The justification sentence is the most valuable line in the email and must never be cut for length** (White et al., *Marketing Letters* 2008: heavy personalisation produces active resistance unless the fit is explicitly justified). **The problem is repetition, not workload** — "you're busy" is true of every business alive and sells nothing, while "you're answering questions you've already answered" is the exact thing the product removes, so moves 3 and 4 are one sentence seen from both ends. **The problem line states a condition of firms that size, never a claim about this firm**, so `ASSERTS_THEIR_PAIN` rejects the rest. And **no offer asks for his calendar**: Gong's 304k emails put an interest CTA at 12% replies against 7% for a time request, and Bay's 85M put a specific offer 4x above a bare interest question, so the ask is one offer on every tier, *"want us to take your last bid and show you how much of the next one is already written?"*, two-stepped so the first reply costs him nothing.

**An offer a stranger can't picture is worth nothing.** A draft offered "the one page we put together on {competitor} against you", which named an artefact nobody had asked for and nobody could describe. The next one offered to show him the reuse in his own last bid, which was better but still described *our process*. **Every ask is conditional and the "if" is load-bearing.** "You're losing bids to the paperwork" tells a stranger what's wrong with his business; "If you're losing bids to the paperwork" hands him the choice. Same words, opposite effect, so `asserts_their_pain()` checks the eight characters in front of the match rather than the words alone.
