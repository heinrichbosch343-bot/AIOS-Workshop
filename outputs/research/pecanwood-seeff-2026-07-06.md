## 1. The estate (verified)

- On-site: **Pecanwood College** (grade 000 to matric), boat club with jetties and a row of **boat garages** (a niche asset class that trades at ~R340k), three restaurants, gym, tennis, 25 m pool, spa, playschool.
- **HOA runs everything**: security, roads, gardens, its own water and sewer plant. Recently the HOA also **leased the golf course and clubhouse from Country Heights** (the Malaysian developer) and pushed through a unified higher levy that gives every homeowner club access. New estate manager appointed.
- Price band: houses R2.85M to R13M, most stock sits R3M to R4.5M. Rentals R28k to R38k per month.
- Location economics: 45 min to Pretoria Central via N4. Buyer profile is affluent families (college on-site), boating and golf people, and Gauteng semigration-lite.

## 3. What her working life probably looks like (inference, grounded in the above)

A two-person office carrying 50+ mandates works like this:

- **She is the product.** Valuations, mandate pitches, viewings, negotiations, OTPs, seller hand-holding: all her. Nicole absorbs paperwork, but one admin against 56 listings and a rentals book is thin.
- **Commission-only.** SA residential commission at this price band is typically 4% to 6% plus VAT, split with the Seeff franchise. A R4M sale might leave her R60k to R100k before tax, and transfers take 8 to 12 weeks after signature, so income arrives in lumpy waves months after the work.
- **Weekends aren't hers.** Show days, buyer viewings arranged around gate access, boat club small talk that doubles as pipeline. Living where you farm means never being off duty.
- **Her phone is the CRM.** WhatsApp threads with sellers, buyers, the HOA office, conveyancers, bond originators (Seeff pushes ooba), photographers. Most follow-up lives in her head.
- **Every listing is a content burden.** Photos, copy, price changes, and status updates across Seeff's site, Property24 and Private Property, times 56, plus keeping sellers feeling like something is happening.
- **Every sale drags an admin tail**: FICA packs from both sides, bond suspensive conditions (typically 21 to 30 days), levy clearance from the HOA, compliance certificates, conveyancer chasing, weekly "any news?" calls from both parties.

## 4. The problems (researched)

### The compliance squeeze (big and current)
- The **FIC is actively cracking down** on estate agencies as accountable institutions. Inspections now demand a working Risk Management and Compliance Programme (RMCP), client due diligence files, **source-of-funds verification**, sanctions screening evidence and timestamped audit trails, on demand.
- Penalties reach **R7.8M**; a real agency (Capital Point Properties) took a **R266k fine** for compliance that "existed only on paper".
- **No FFC, no legal right to earn commission.** FFC renewal is tied to being compliant. For a two-person branch, this is a genuine existential admin load with zero revenue attached.

### The lead leak
- Portal enquiries (Property24, Private Property, seeff.com) arrive around the clock. Industry-wide pattern: **contacted once, then forgotten**, while buyers actually take **3 to 6 months** to transact. Follow-up discipline, not lead volume, is the constraint.

### Mandate retention (the spearhead — and it's a contested market, see section 2 correction)
- Pecanwood is a genuine three-way fight (Pam Golding 44, Seeff 42, RE/MAX 35 on Property24). A sole mandate is time-boxed; when it lapses, an unhappy seller has two big-brand alternatives one call away. Retention is real money at risk.
- The defence has always been a steady drumbeat of seller feedback (portal stats, viewing feedback, market movement). The honest framing: **most agents don't do this at all** because it doesn't scale by hand across 40+ mandates. The opportunity isn't "save Louise time on calls she makes today" (unverified assumption); it's "let her out-service Pam Golding and RE/MAX at zero time cost, doing the thing none of them bother to do."

### Deal-in-progress chaos
- Between OTP and transfer: bond deadlines, FICA collection, levy clearance, compliance certificates, conveyancer milestones. All tracked manually, all generating anxious calls she has to field.

### Slow, thin market
- ~6% of the estate on the market with stock sitting (some listings date back to 2024). High levies after the unified-levy change may push more owners to sell, but buyers at R4M are rate-sensitive. Long sales cycles amplify every other problem.

## 5. Where BoschAI fits

### The flagship: the Pecanwood Property Brain ("connect all the houses")
She sits inside a **closed universe of 880 known assets** and already touches most transactions. Nobody is better placed to own the data layer of an entire suburb:

- A registry of every stand: last sale price and date, owner tenure, size, rental status, mandate history (deeds data via Lightstone-type feeds plus her own records).
- **Likely-seller scoring**: long tenure + life-stage signals + rental conversions + levy pressure = who sells next. She knocks on doors with "I have a buyer for homes like yours" before the mandate exists.
- **Buyer-to-house matching**: every enquiry becomes a stored buyer profile matched against all 880 homes, not just the 50 listed ones. Off-market deals are pure mandate generation.
- A Johan-style voice/dashboard front end makes this filmable and demo-able ("Johan, which owners in Pecanwood have held longer than 15 years and enquired about rentals?").
- Moat: once this exists, no competing agency can enter Pecanwood. It also becomes the reference build to sell to every estate-based agency in the country (every golf estate has a Louise).

### The supporting systems (each one a known agency pain)
1. **Lead nurture bot** — instant response to every portal enquiry, qualification, 6-month WhatsApp/email nurture, viewing scheduling that handles estate gate access. Fixes the leak with zero extra hours.
2. **Seller feedback autopilot** — weekly per-mandate report (portal views, enquiries, viewings, comparable movement) sent under her name. Directly protects the sole-mandate book, which is her entire stock.
3. **Deal pipeline watchdog** — tracks every OTP's suspensive conditions, FICA status, levy clearance and conveyancer milestones; nudges the responsible party; sends both clients a weekly plain-English status note. Kills the "any news?" call volume. (Same pattern as the Pipeline Watchdog already on the build list.)
4. **FICA/RMCP compliance robot** — per-deal document collection portal, checklist, and a timestamped audit trail that makes an FIC inspection a non-event. Sellable as "audit-proof" given the current crackdown; this is the one with fear-driven urgency.
5. **Listing content machine** — photos in, everything out: portal copy, social posts, brochure, WhatsApp broadcast to the buyer database. 56 listings' worth of content upkeep, automated.
6. **New-resident concierge** — every buyer gets a branded Pecanwood onboarding pack (levies, boat club, college, building rules, contractor rules). Cheap goodwill that seeds the next decade of referrals and rentals.

### Sequencing recommendation
Start with **2 (seller feedback) or 3 (pipeline watchdog)**: small, fast to demo with her real data, and they touch money she's already earned. The **Property Brain** is the second conversation, once trust exists, and it's the one worth a retainer. **Compliance (4)** is the wedge if she ever mentions FIC or audit stress; it sells itself right now.

### Discovery questions for the first conversation
1. "Walk me through what happens after an offer is signed. Who chases what?"
2. "How do you keep 50 sellers feeling looked after at the same time?"
3. "What happens to a Property24 enquiry that doesn't answer your first call?"
4. "How long does a FICA pack take per deal, and who assembles it?"
5. "If you knew which owners were likely to sell next year, what would that be worth?"
